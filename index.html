Having tumor-reactive T cells present is not the same as having them function well. The Lee Lab’s liver-metastasis work makes that distinction especially important: an immune response at one site can be suppressed by a tumor somewhere else.

I built a small evidence-to-panel tool to ask: which measured changes recur across different interventions, and what should we record to test them again? It connects the lab’s published observations to a transparent readout shortlist and a specimen-record template.

How it works

The inputs are individual endpoint values from Lee et al., 2020, Table S1. I compare four markers shared by four contrasts, keeping the parent cell population fixed. A candidate must decrease with liver involvement and increase in each of three separate intervention comparisons.

The starting cutoff is five percentage points, chosen for exploration—not a validated biological threshold. The optional sensitivity check asks whether the cutoff still holds after omitting any one observation. These are descriptive comparisons within one paper, not independent validation or a treatment ranking.

Why this matters for the lab

The lab studies how organ context shapes systemic immunity and therapy response. A useful follow-up question is whether a change in tumor-reactive cell abundance is accompanied by a change in their state. The two need not move together.

A prospective comparison could retain an abundance anchor plus the shortlisted endpoints, with matched tissue-site and processing records, appropriate staining and stimulation controls, and blinded analysis. If a state change persists within batches without an abundance shift, that would support a state-focused interpretation; if it disappears after accounting for batch or sample quality, the next step is measurement review. Neither result alone identifies a causal mechanism. This is an inferred opportunity, not a claim about the lab’s internal priorities.

Methods note

Source: Lee JC et al., Science Immunology (2020), doi:10.1126/sciimmunol.aba0759. Table S1 was retrieved from the BioStudies archive. Only Fig 3E/F/H/I and 4D/G endpoints shown here were extracted. The workbook has 15 values per group for Fig 3F; the figure caption’s general n=10 statement differs. Actual nonempty cells are used.

Assumptions, limits and reproducibility

Groups are compared separately; marker percentages use KSP-tetramer+ CD8+ cells as the denominator, while abundance uses all CD8+ cells. No cross-figure animal matching or correlation is inferred. No batch or stable animal IDs, raw FCS files, gate-level counts or compensation information are available in this extract. The 400 endpoint records may reuse the same animals across markers.

Dots are published observations, horizontal lines are means. Differences are percentage points, not relative percentage changes. Leave-one-observation ranges are sensitivity ranges, not confidence intervals; they cannot assess between-experiment reproducibility. Medians are shown as a complementary outlier check. No values are excluded, no p-values are recomputed, and no equivalence claim is made for abundance.

The shared-marker subset was selected for complete coverage, and the directional rule is informed by this same paper. This is retrospective, exploratory readout prioritization—not independent validation, a predictive biomarker, an optimal panel, or a new mechanism. Separate treatment contrasts are not exchangeable and are never pooled. Functional cytokine measurements involve stimulation; phenotype and cytokine records must preserve that distinction.

Analysis and interface were developed with AI assistance and checked against source cells. This project does not establish independent bench execution or coding proficiency. Original data remain the authors’ work.
